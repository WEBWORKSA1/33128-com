Probate in Miami-Dade: What Happens After Someone Dies

A clear introduction to Florida probate for families: depositing the will, summary vs. formal administration, the personal representative's role, creditors and homestead.

By the 33128.com editorial team · Updated November 2026 · 7 min read

Probate is the court-supervised process of gathering a deceased person's assets, paying valid debts and transferring what's left to the right people. In Miami-Dade, probate filings, certified copies and wills for deposit are handled on the 14th floor of the Lawson E. Thomas Courthouse Center (175 NW 1st Ave., 33128).

Step one: deposit the original will

Florida law requires whoever has custody of a will to deposit it with the clerk of the county where the person lived within 10 days after learning of the death. Copies aren't enough — the court needs the original.

Summary vs. formal administration

Summary administrationFormal administration
For smaller estates — non-exempt assets of $75,000 or less, or when the person died more than two years agoThe standard process for larger or more complex estates
No personal representative appointed; faster and cheaperCourt appoints a personal representative (executor) who manages the estate
Often finished in weeks to a few monthsCommonly 6–12 months or longer

Do you need a lawyer?

In Florida, a personal representative in formal administration generally must be represented by an attorney (with narrow exceptions). Attorney fees are often set by agreement or by a statutory schedule based on estate value.

Creditors and the notice period

The personal representative publishes a notice to creditors and serves known creditors. Claims filed after the deadline are generally barred — one of the main protections probate provides to heirs.

Florida homestead

Florida's homestead rules are unique: a primary residence may be protected from most creditors and may have restrictions on who can inherit it when a spouse or minor child survives. Homestead questions are a frequent reason families hire probate counsel.

Assets that skip probate

Jointly owned property with survivorship rights, accounts with a payable-on-death beneficiary, life insurance and retirement accounts with named beneficiaries, and assets in a living trust usually pass outside probate.

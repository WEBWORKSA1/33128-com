Got a Traffic Ticket in Miami-Dade? Your 30-Day Options Explained

Pay, take a driving course or contest it? A clear explanation of your options for a Florida civil traffic citation, points, insurance impact and when a criminal traffic charge needs a lawyer.

By the 33128.com editorial team · Updated November 2026 · 6 min read

A Florida civil traffic citation gives you 30 days to choose how to respond. Missing that window can lead to a driver's license suspension, so act early.

Your three main options

OptionWhat happensBest when
Pay the fineYou're adjudicated guilty; points are added to your recordLow-point violation and clean record
Elect a Basic Driver Improvement coursePay the fine, complete an approved course; adjudication is withheld and points are not assessedMoving violations where points would raise insurance; limited number of elections allowed
Contest (request a hearing)A judge or hearing officer hears the caseYou have a real defense or the stakes (points, CDL) are high

Why points matter

Florida suspends licenses when drivers accumulate too many points within set periods (for example, 12 points in 12 months). Even a few points can raise insurance premiums for years. The driver-course election exists precisely to keep points off your record — but you can only use it a limited number of times.

When it's not just a ticket

Some traffic offenses are criminal, not civil: driving on a suspended license, DUI, reckless driving, leaving the scene of a crash. These require a court appearance and can carry jail time and a criminal record. Talk to a criminal-defense attorney before your first court date. In Miami-Dade, many criminal traffic matters are heard at the Richard E. Gerstein Justice Building (1351 NW 12th St.).

How to contest effectively

  • Request the hearing within the 30-day window.
  • Gather evidence: photos of the intersection and signs, dash-cam footage, witness contact information.
  • Be polite and factual. If the officer doesn't appear, the case may be dismissed — but don't count on it.

Commercial drivers and anyone close to a suspension should consider a traffic attorney; the cost is often less than years of higher premiums. Ask for a free review.

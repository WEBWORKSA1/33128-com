Family Court in Miami: Divorce, Custody and Support Basics

What to expect at the Lawson E. Thomas Courthouse Center: Florida's no-fault divorce, residency requirement, parenting plans, child support guidelines, mediation and the parenting course.

By the 33128.com editorial team · Updated November 2026 · 8 min read

Family matters in Miami-Dade — divorce (called "dissolution of marriage" in Florida), paternity, custody, child support and domestic-violence injunctions — are centered at the Lawson E. Thomas Courthouse Center, 175 NW 1st Avenue (33128). Domestic-violence filings are handled on the mezzanine level.

Divorce in Florida: the essentials

  • No-fault. You only need to show the marriage is "irretrievably broken" (or that a spouse has been mentally incapacitated for at least three years).
  • Residency. At least one spouse must have lived in Florida for six months before filing.
  • Simplified dissolution is available when both spouses agree, have no minor children, and settle property between themselves — no lawyers required in many cases.
  • Equitable distribution. Marital assets and debts are divided fairly — which usually starts at equal but can be adjusted.

Children: time-sharing and parenting plans

Florida no longer uses the terms "custody" and "visitation." Instead, parents share parental responsibility and follow a court-approved parenting plan with a time-sharing schedule. Courts decide based on the best interests of the child. Parents in a divorce with minor children are typically required to complete a parent education course before final judgment.

Child support and alimony

Child support follows statutory guidelines based on both parents' incomes, the number of overnights each parent has and costs like health insurance and child care. Florida's 2023 alimony reform eliminated permanent alimony and set new limits on duration; bridge-the-gap, rehabilitative and durational alimony remain.

Mediation

Most contested family cases must attend mediation before trial. A neutral mediator helps you reach agreement; anything you sign can become a binding order. Prepare by knowing your budget, your priorities and your bottom line.

Domestic violence

If you're in danger, call 911. Petitions for injunctions for protection can be filed at the courthouse, and advocates are available to help. There is no filing fee for a domestic-violence injunction petition.

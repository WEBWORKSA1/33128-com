Jury Duty in Miami: State vs. Federal Summons and What to Expect

Received a jury summons for downtown Miami? Learn the difference between state and federal jury service, how to request a postponement, what a typical day looks like and how pay works.

By the 33128.com editorial team · Updated November 2026 · 6 min read

Two different court systems can summon you for jury duty in downtown Miami — and they run separate processes. Figuring out which one sent your summons is step one.

State or federal?

State (Miami-Dade County)Federal (Southern District of Florida)
Who sends itClerk of the Court for Miami-Dade CountyU.S. District Court jury office
Typical casesCivil disputes, state crimesFederal crimes, federal civil cases
Where you reportLocation printed on the summonsWilkie D. Ferguson, Jr. U.S. Courthouse, 400 N. Miami Ave (33128) for Miami
LengthOften one day or one trialCan involve a term of on-call service

The summons letterhead tells you which court sent it. Follow that court's instructions — online questionnaires, call-in numbers and postponement rules differ.

Postponements and excusals

Both systems allow you to request a postponement for conflicts such as travel, exams or medical appointments, and both have specific excusal categories set by law or court policy. Request it before your reporting date using the method on the summons. Ignoring a summons is not an option: courts can issue orders to show cause to people who fail to appear.

What a typical day looks like

  1. Check in at the jury assembly room after security screening.
  2. Orientation — usually a short video explaining the jury's role. The U.S. Courts' own overview video is in our video library.
  3. Waiting — bring something to read. You may wait hours before being called.
  4. Voir dire — the judge and lawyers ask a panel questions to select an impartial jury. Answer honestly; it's not a test.
  5. Selected or released. Many people are released the same day.

Pay and your employer

Jurors receive a modest daily fee set by law, and federal jurors may be reimbursed for certain travel and parking costs. Florida law protects employees from being fired or threatened for serving on a jury; federal law gives similar protection for federal service. Ask the jury office for a certificate of attendance to give your employer.

Practical tips

  • Take Metrorail to Government Center — then the Inner Loop Metromover one stop for the federal courthouse.
  • Wear layers; jury rooms are cold.
  • Do not research the case, visit the scene or post about it online. Judges take this seriously.
Serving on a jury is one of the few direct ways citizens shape justice. Most people who serve say the experience was better than they expected.

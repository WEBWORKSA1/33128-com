Arrested or Charged in Miami-Dade? The First 72 Hours Explained

First appearance, bond, the public defender, arraignment and the most common mistakes people make right after an arrest in Miami-Dade County.

By the 33128.com editorial team · Updated November 2026 · 7 min read

An arrest is disorienting for the person charged and their family. Understanding the first steps helps you avoid mistakes that are hard to undo.

First appearance

Florida requires that a person who is arrested and held be brought before a judge within 24 hours. At this hearing the judge reviews probable cause, sets release conditions or bond, and advises the person of the right to counsel.

Bond and pretrial release

Release can be on recognizance, on a cash or surety bond, or with conditions such as pretrial supervision or monitoring. Some offenses follow a standard bond schedule; others require a hearing. Family members can usually post bond through the corrections department or a licensed bail bond agent.

Right to a lawyer

If you can't afford a lawyer, the court can appoint the Public Defender. If you hire private counsel, do it early — the first days are when evidence is preserved, witnesses are located and charging decisions can be influenced.

The most common mistakes

  • Talking about the case — to police, cellmates or on recorded jail phone lines. Jail calls are recorded.
  • Posting on social media.
  • Contacting the alleged victim, especially when a no-contact order is in place.
  • Missing a court date, which can lead to a warrant and bond forfeiture.

What comes next

After formal charges are filed, there is an arraignment where a plea is entered, followed by discovery, motions, plea negotiations and, if needed, trial. Diversion programs may be available for some first-time or low-level offenses.

Federal charges

Federal criminal cases in Miami are heard at the Wilkie D. Ferguson, Jr. U.S. Courthouse (400 N. Miami Ave., 33128) and follow different rules, including federal sentencing guidelines. Federal cases demand counsel with federal experience.

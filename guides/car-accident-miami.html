After a Car Accident in Miami: Florida's No-Fault Rules, the 14-Day Window and Deadlines

What to do after a Miami crash: Florida PIP, the 14-day medical treatment rule, modified comparative negligence, the two-year deadline and how injury claims are valued.

By the 33128.com editorial team · Updated November 2026 · 9 min read

Miami-Dade has some of the busiest roads in Florida. If you're hurt in a crash, a few early decisions — especially in the first two weeks — have an outsized effect on what you can recover.

At the scene

  1. Check for injuries and call 911. Florida requires crashes involving injury, death or significant property damage to be reported.
  2. Photograph vehicles, plates, the scene, traffic signals and your injuries.
  3. Exchange driver, insurance and registration details; get witness names and numbers.
  4. Don't admit fault or speculate — stick to facts.

Florida no-fault insurance (PIP)

Florida drivers must carry $10,000 in Personal Injury Protection. Your own PIP pays a share of medical bills and lost wages regardless of who caused the crash.

The 14-day rule: to use PIP benefits, you generally must receive initial medical treatment within 14 days of the accident. Get checked even if you feel fine — some injuries show up later.

Stepping outside no-fault

To sue the at-fault driver for pain and suffering, Florida law requires a serious injury threshold — such as significant and permanent loss of a bodily function, permanent injury, significant scarring or disfigurement. Economic losses beyond PIP can also be pursued.

Comparative negligence changed in 2023

Since March 2023, Florida uses modified comparative negligence: if you're found more than 50% at fault, you generally recover nothing. At 50% or less, your recovery is reduced by your percentage of fault. Medical-negligence cases are treated differently.

The deadline

For negligence claims arising after March 24, 2023, the statute of limitations in Florida is generally two years (it was four years before). Missing it usually ends your claim permanently.

How claims are valued

Insurers consider medical expenses, future treatment, lost income, property damage and non-economic damages (pain, suffering, loss of enjoyment of life). A rough teaching method is the "multiplier" approach — try our claim illustrator — but real values depend on liability, policy limits and evidence.

Do you need an injury lawyer?

Most injury attorneys work on contingency — no fee unless they recover money, typically a percentage set by Florida Bar rules. For anything beyond minor soft-tissue claims, a consultation costs nothing and can protect against early lowball offers.

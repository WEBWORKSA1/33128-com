Small Claims Court in Miami-Dade: How to Sue (or Defend) for $8,000 or Less

A plain-English walkthrough of Florida small claims for Miami-Dade residents: the $8,000 limit, where to file, serving the defendant, the pretrial conference and what to bring.

By the 33128.com editorial team · Updated November 2026 · 8 min read

Florida does not have a separate "small claims court." Instead, the County Court handles disputes of $8,000 or less (not counting costs, interest and attorney's fees) under simplified Small Claims Rules. It's designed so ordinary people can resolve a broken contract, unpaid invoice, security-deposit fight or minor car-damage claim without a lawyer.

Is your case a good fit?

  • The amount is $8,000 or less — or you're willing to give up anything above that.
  • You can show what happened with documents: receipts, contracts, texts, photos, estimates.
  • The defendant can actually pay. Winning a judgment and collecting it are two different things.

Above $8,000, cases up to $50,000 stay in County Court under regular civil rules; larger claims go to Circuit Court.

Where to file

File in the county where the defendant lives, where the problem happened, or where the property is located. In Miami-Dade, civil filings are handled by the Clerk of the Court and Comptroller; the historic courthouse at 73 W. Flagler Street (ZIP 33130) is the central civil location. Many filings can also be made through the Florida Courts E-Filing Portal.

Step by step

  1. Send a demand letter. Give the other side a final chance to pay with a clear deadline. Judges like to see you tried.
  2. Complete the Statement of Claim. Name the defendant correctly — for a business, look up its exact registered name on the Florida Division of Corporations (Sunbiz).
  3. Pay the filing fee, which scales with the amount claimed. Fee waivers exist for people who qualify as indigent.
  4. Serve the defendant through the sheriff or a certified process server. The case cannot move forward until service is completed.
  5. Attend the pretrial conference. This first hearing is where many cases settle, often through mediation the same day. If you don't show up, your case can be dismissed (or a default entered against you if you're the defendant).
  6. Trial. If there's no settlement, the judge sets a trial date. Bring three copies of every document and any witnesses.

If you've been sued

Read the summons immediately and note the pretrial conference date. Show up. Bring your evidence and any counterclaim you have. If you believe you owe part of the money, mediation at the pretrial conference may be your best outcome.

Collecting a judgment

If the defendant doesn't pay voluntarily, Florida provides tools such as a fact information sheet, writs of execution, bank garnishments and judgment liens. Collection can take longer than the lawsuit itself — another reason to weigh whether the defendant has assets.

Tip: Organize your evidence into a one-page timeline with exhibits labeled A, B, C. Judges hear dozens of cases a day; clarity wins.

Visiting the Courthouses in 33128: Security, Parking, Transit and What to Wear

A practical first-timer's guide to the downtown Miami courthouse district — security screening, Metrorail and parking, interpreters, accessibility and courtroom etiquette.

By the 33128.com editorial team · Updated November 2026 · 7 min read

ZIP code 33128 is small — well under half a square mile — but it holds an unusual concentration of courts. Federal judges, bankruptcy trustees, family-court magistrates and probate clerks all work within a few blocks of the Government Center Metrorail station. If you have a hearing, a summons or a filing to make, this guide covers everything that happens before you reach the courtroom.

1. Confirm which building you need

The single biggest mistake first-time visitors make is going to the wrong courthouse. Federal matters (including federal jury service) are on North Miami Avenue; family and probate matters are at the Lawson E. Thomas Courthouse Center on NW 1st Avenue; deeds and official records are recorded at Courthouse East on NW 1st Street; and general civil suits are filed at the historic county courthouse on West Flagler Street just across the ZIP line. Read your notice carefully — it lists the building, floor and courtroom. Our courthouse directory and the "Which building?" finder translate case types into addresses.

2. Security screening

Every courthouse here uses airport-style screening: walk-through magnetometers and X-ray belts for bags. Lines are longest between 8:15 and 9:15 a.m., when most calendars start. Plan on 20–45 minutes on a busy morning.

  • Leave at home: knives of any size, scissors, pepper spray, multitools, tools and anything that could be used as a weapon.
  • Bring: a government-issued photo ID and your paperwork.
  • Phones: generally allowed in public areas but must be silenced; photography and recording in courtrooms are restricted unless a judge permits them. Federal courthouses have their own electronic-device rules — check the court's website before you go.

3. Getting there: Metrorail beats driving

Government Center station sits underneath the Stephen P. Clark Center and connects Metrorail with the free Metromover loops. Riders heading to the federal courthouse can take the Inner Loop Metromover one stop to the Wilkie D. Ferguson, Jr. station. Most 33128 buildings are a five-minute walk from the station, and downtown Miami is one of the most walkable areas in South Florida.

If you must drive, budget for garage parking and give yourself extra time — street meters fill early and time limits are strict. Federal jurors should follow the parking instructions printed on their summons, which may include reimbursement.

4. What to wear and how to behave

Dress as you would for a job interview: clean, conservative clothing; no hats in the courtroom; no clothing with slogans. Stand when the judge enters, address the judge as "Your Honor," speak only when it's your turn, and never interrupt. Bring a sweater — courtrooms are famously cold.

5. Interpreters and accessibility

Miami is one of the most multilingual court systems in the country. If you or a witness needs an interpreter, ask the clerk or the court's interpreter office well before your hearing date. Requests for disability accommodations under the ADA should also be made in advance; your summons or notice usually names the office to contact and the deadline.

6. Children

Many courtrooms do not allow young children unless they are part of the case. Arrange child care in advance. Family-court visitors should check whether a supervised waiting area is available.

7. What the clerk can and cannot do

Clerks can explain procedures, accept filings, give you forms and tell you fees. By law they cannot give legal advice — they can't tell you which form to choose for your situation, what to argue or whether you'll win. For that you need an attorney or a legal-aid program.

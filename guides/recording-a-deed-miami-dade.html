How to Record a Deed in Miami-Dade County

Recording deeds and official records at Courthouse East (22 NW 1st St.): Florida formatting rules, witnesses, notarization, documentary stamp tax and e-recording.

By the 33128.com editorial team · Updated November 2026 · 6 min read

Recording a deed puts the world on notice of who owns a property. In Miami-Dade, the County Recorder's Office is on the first floor of Courthouse East, 22 NW 1st Street (33128), and many documents can also be e-recorded through approved vendors.

Florida formatting requirements

  • A blank space of 3 inches by 3 inches at the top right of the first page, and 1 inch by 3 inches on following pages, for the clerk's recording information.
  • Name and address of the person who prepared the document.
  • Legible names of each person who signed, witnessed or notarized it.
  • The grantee's (new owner's) mailing address.

Witnesses and notary

Florida deeds conveying real property must be signed in the presence of two subscribing witnesses and notarized. Witness names and addresses should be printed beneath their signatures. Remote online notarization is legal in Florida when done through a registered provider.

Documentary stamp tax

Florida charges documentary stamp tax on deeds based on the consideration paid. Miami-Dade uses a different rate structure from the rest of the state, including a surtax on many transfers that are not single-family residences. Check the current rates with the Clerk or the Florida Department of Revenue before recording.

After recording

Recorded documents become searchable public records. Homeowners should then file for the homestead exemption with the Property Appraiser by the annual deadline if the home is their primary residence.

Watch out for title fraud. Miami-Dade offers free property-fraud alert services that notify owners when documents are recorded in their name — worth signing up for.
